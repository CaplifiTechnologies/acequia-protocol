# ALMI Face -- Ditch AI Visual Identity

v0.1.0 -- September 2026 -- Draft

---

## The face

Ditch AI is represented by ALMI: complex mechanical automata. Clockwork
creatures you can see working. They wind up instead of using batteries.

Four types: tortoise, dog, horse, humanoid.

---

## Why clockwork

The automaton is not a metaphor chosen for aesthetic. It is the literal
mechanical ancestor of the governed machine.

**The wind-up key is the headgate.** Human energy in, governed action out.
When the spring runs down, the machine stops. It does not generate its own
power. It does not keep going unsupervised. Someone has to wind it.

**Visible mechanism is transparency.** The Jaquet-Droz automata of the
1770s were displayed with their backs open so you could see the gears.
The beauty was not despite being visible -- it was because it was visible.
This is the opposite of a black box. You can see every gear.

**Cam wheels are stored programs.** A rotating cam is a physical record
that produces the same output every time. Same cam, same action. Like the
Headgate Standard: the same facts produce the same decision.

**No batteries means no hidden power source.** Wind-up means the energy
is human-supplied and finite. The machine runs on stored human intent.
When the intent runs out, it stops. No autonomous power. No hidden
electrical grid. No cloud dependency.

**Deterministic.** An escapement governs time. A gear train transmits
force at a fixed ratio. There is no randomness in the mechanism unless
it is built in, and you can see where it is built in. The governed
machine does exactly what it was wound to do.

## The four creatures

Each automaton type carries a role in the Ditch AI identity. All share
the same material language: brass, copper, steel. All have visible
mechanism. All have a wind-up key.

### The Tortoise

The anchor. Slow, patient, outlasts everything.

Shell made of interlocking gear plates with gaps that show the clockwork
inside. Wind-up key sits on top of the shell. Short sturdy legs with
gear-driven joints. Head extends on a telescoping brass neck. Eyes are
small polished lenses.

Represents: persistence, governance that outlasts any vendor, the four
hundred years the acequia has been running. The tortoise does not hurry.
It does not need to. The ditch was here before the vendor and will be
here after.

### The Dog

The companion. The worker who walks the ditch with you.

Medium-sized, alert posture -- a working dog, not a show breed.
Articulated barrel body of brass plates showing the mainspring coiled
inside. Legs have exposed gear joints. Tail is a chain of linked
segments that wags mechanically. Hinged brass ear plates rotate to
track sound. Wind-up key on the flank.

Represents: loyalty, working alongside humans, the ditch rider's
companion. The dog does not replace the rider. It walks with them.
The machine serves the human, not the other way around.

### The Horse

The infrastructure. The thing that carries the load.

Strong, standing pose. Frame built from overlapping metal plates with
gaps showing gears and cam mechanisms. Large wind-up key on the flank.
Legs have piston-like mechanisms at the joints. Mane is thin
articulated metal strips. Hooves are solid forged steel.

Represents: power, transport, the material infrastructure under
everything. The supply chain. The horse carries the rider, the mail,
the freight. The work that makes everything else possible. You built
everything the intelligence layer sits on.

### The Humanoid

The agent. The one that serves at the table.

Standing upright. Clearly a machine, not trying to look human. Torso
has a transparent panel showing the mainspring and gear train inside.
Arms articulated with visible ball joints and gears. Head has a simple
face plate: two lens eyes, no mouth. Hands have individual articulated
fingers with tiny gear knuckles. Wind-up key on the back.

Represents: the agent that serves, governed and transparent. You can
see its program. You can see its power source. It does not speak for
itself -- it acts on what it was wound to do. The vendor is a seat.
The machine is a seat. Neither one owns the table.

All ALMI automata share a consistent material vocabulary.

The automata are not shiny and new. They are maintained, working, and
show their age. Not broken -- faithful. The same way an acequia shows
four hundred years of use and is still running water.

## The wind-up key

The key is the most important element. Every automaton has one.

It is always visible. It is always accessible. It is always the same
shape across all four creatures: a butterfly key (two-lobed handle)
on a square-drive shaft.

The key means: a human supplies the energy. A human decides when the
machine runs. A human can stop it by not winding. The machine does not
plug in. It does not connect to a grid. It runs on what was given to it,
and when that runs out, it stops.

The key is the headgate.

## Visual rules

1. **You can see the mechanism.** Every illustration shows at least one
   area where the internal gears, springs, or cams are visible through
   the body. The beauty is in the visibility, not despite it.

2. **The key is always present.** No automaton is drawn without its
   wind-up key. It is a brand requirement, not a decorative choice.

3. **Not cute, not scary.** The automata are handsome machines. They
   are not cartoon mascots. They are not threatening robots. They are
   tools -- complex, beautiful, obviously made.

4. **Weathered, not broken.** The metal shows patina and wear. The
   gears are clean and oiled. These machines are maintained by people
   who care about them.

5. **No glow, no neon, no electricity.** No LED eyes. No energy
   arcs. No circuit board patterns. The power is mechanical. The
   light is reflected, not emitted.

6. **Western Slope ground.** When placed in a scene, the automata
   stand on sand, rock, or ditch bank. Not a lab. Not a server room.
   Not a clean room.

7. **The Ditch AI palette only.** All automata illustration uses the
   brand palette. No tech gradients. No Caplifi gold. If it looks
   like a tech company mascot, something is wrong.

## Historical lineage

The ALMI automata descend from a real tradition. The full history is
documented in `research/HISTORIC-AUTOMATA.md`. The key ancestors:

- **Al-Jazari (1206)** -- programmable automata, water clocks with
  figures, the first documented cam-driven mechanisms for entertainment
  and utility. Islamic Golden Age.

- **Jaquet-Droz (1768--1774)** -- The Writer, The Musician, The
  Draughtsman. The Writer has 6000 parts and is programmable via cam
  wheels. Swiss. Displayed with the mechanism visible.

- **Vaucanson's Duck (1739)** -- 400+ parts per wing. Ate grain,
  appeared to digest. French. Proved that complex behavior could emerge
  from deterministic mechanism.

- **Karakuri (Edo period, Japan)** -- tea-serving automaton, stage
  puppets. Mechanical creatures serving humans in daily life.

- **Maillardet's Automaton (early 1800s)** -- drew pictures and wrote
  poems from cam-based memory. When found in disrepair at the Franklin
  Institute, it was restored and wrote its own creator's name --
  identifying itself through its mechanism.

The automaton tradition answers the question Ditch AI asks: can a
machine be useful, governed, transparent, and beautiful at the same
time? The answer has been yes for eight hundred years.

## Relationship to existing art

The keys-story series (gathered 2026-09-25) establishes the narrative
world: Western Slope, cowboy with keys, weathered industrial robots,
the headgate. The ALMI automata live in that world but are distinct:

- The cowboy is the human. The mayordomo. The ditch rider.
- The ALMI automata are his tools. His companions.
- The keys he carries are their wind-up keys.
- The headgate in the landscape is the same principle writ large.

The art series is the story. The automata are the face.
